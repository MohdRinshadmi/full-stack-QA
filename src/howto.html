How to use this book

1 · Speak the green box

Every question opens with an Interview answer block: 20–40 seconds, spoken aloud. Memorise the shape, not the words. In a real round, say this, stop, and let the interviewer pull you deeper.

2 · Own the blue box

The Deep dive is what you say when they ask "why?" or "how does that actually work?". If you can teach it, you know it.

3 · Land the purple box

The Senior angle is the sentence that separates a 2-year engineer from a 5-year one: trade-offs, failure modes, cost, operability. This is what gets you levelled up.

4 · Rehearse the amber box

Interviewers rarely accept the first answer. The follow-up is the question that is actually being scored. Practise the transition until it feels automatic.

Interview answer
The 20–40 second spoken answer. Concise, confident, no hedging.
Deep dive
The mechanism. What is actually happening in the engine, the kernel, the planner or the network.
Senior angle
Trade-offs, failure modes, when not to do it, and what it costs in production.
Production example
A concrete situation from a real system — the story you tell to make the answer stick.
Code / Architecture
Copy-ready code or an ASCII architecture sketch you can reproduce on a whiteboard.
Likely follow-up + answer
The next question, and how to answer it without losing momentum.
Red flags
Answers that silently cost you the offer. Never say these.

A note on your résumé numbers

You claim a 35% API response-time reduction, a 60%+ Vite build-time reduction, 40+ REST APIs, 2,000+ monthly payment transactions across Stripe/PayPal/Razorpay, and a stateless WebSocket fan-out server with Redis Pub/Sub and Yjs CRDT sync. Assume every one of those is a question. For each, be ready with: how you measured it (tool + baseline), what the bottleneck was (specific), what you changed (specific), and how you proved it (the after-number, and how you knew it held). Parts 2 and 4 drill exactly these.
